1. Units, Measurements, and Kinematics
* Units and Measurements: The comparison of a physical quantity with its standard unit is called measurement 1. There are seven fundamental units: metre (length), kilogram (mass), second (time), kelvin (temperature), ampere (current), candela (luminous intensity), and mole (amount of substance) 2. Radian and steradian are supplementary units for plane and solid angles, respectively 2, 3. Common systems of units include CGS, FPS, MKS, and the SI system 4, 5. Dimensional formulas represent physical quantities, such as area $L^2$, velocity $LT^{-1}$, force $MLT^{-2}$, and work $ML^2T^{-2}$ 6. In error analysis, the maximum relative error in multiplication or division ($x = a \times b$ or $x = a/b$) is represented as $\Delta x = \pm(\Delta a + \Delta b)$ 7.
* Kinematics: This branch covers motion in a straight line, frame of reference, uniform and non-uniform motion, instantaneous velocity, and uniformly accelerated motion analyzed via position-time and velocity-time graphs 8, 9. Motion in a plane includes the study of scalar and vector quantities, vector resolution, scalar and vector products, projectile motion, and uniform circular motion 9, 10.
2. Mechanics and Rigid Body Dynamics
* Laws of Motion: Focuses on the intuitive concept of force, inertia, Newton's first, second, and third laws of motion, and impulse 10. It includes the law of conservation of linear momentum, equilibrium of concurrent forces, and the dynamics of uniform circular motion, such as a vehicle on a banked road 10, 11. It also covers static, kinetic, and rolling friction, as well as lubrication 10, 11.
* Work, Energy, and Power: Explores work done by constant and variable forces, the work-energy theorem, and power 11. It introduces potential energy (like that of a spring), conservative and non-conservative forces, and elastic and inelastic collisions in one and two dimensions 11.
* Rotational Motion: Central concepts include the centre of mass of two-particle systems and rigid bodies 12. It explores the moment of a force, torque, and the law of conservation of angular momentum ($L = I\omega$) 12, 13. Work done in rotational motion is calculated as Torque $\times$ Angular Displacement 14.
* Gravitation: Explores Kepler's laws of planetary motion, the universal law of gravitation, and the variation of acceleration due to gravity with altitude and depth 15. It also covers gravitational potential energy, escape speed, and orbital velocity of a satellite 15.
3. Properties of Bulk Matter
* Solids: Covers elasticity, stress-strain relationships, Hooke's law, Young’s modulus, bulk modulus, and Poisson's ratio 15, 16.
* Fluids: Includes pressure due to fluid columns, Pascal's law (applied in hydraulic lifts and brakes), and the effect of gravity on fluid pressure 16. Topics also cover viscosity, Stokes' law, terminal velocity, streamline and turbulent flow, Bernoulli's theorem, and surface tension applied to capillary rise, drops, and bubbles 16, 17.
4. Heat and Thermodynamics
* Thermal Properties: Includes thermal expansion of solids, liquids, gases, and the anomalous expansion of water 17. Explores specific heat capacity, calorimetry, latent heat, and modes of heat transfer (conduction, convection, and radiation), along with Wien's displacement law and Stefan's law 17, 18.
* Thermodynamics & Kinetic Theory: Focuses on thermal equilibrium, the zeroth, first, and second laws of thermodynamics, and isothermal, adiabatic, reversible, and irreversible processes 18. The kinetic theory of gases covers the equation of a perfect gas, kinetic interpretation of temperature, RMS speed of gas molecules, degrees of freedom, law of equipartition of energy, and mean free path 19.
5. Oscillations and Waves
* Oscillations: Analyzes periodic motion, frequency, and Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) 19, 20. The general equation for SHM is $x = A \sin(\omega t + \phi)$ 21. Concepts also include the restoring force of a spring, kinetic/potential energy in SHM, and the time period of a simple pendulum 20.
* Waves: Studies transverse and longitudinal waves, speed of a travelling wave, superposition of waves, standing waves in strings and organ pipes, harmonics, and beats 20, 22.
6. Electromagnetism
* Electrostatics: Covers Coulomb's law, continuous charge distributions, and electric dipoles (electric field on the axis is $\frac{2KP}{r^3}$ and on the equatorial position is $\frac{-KP}{r^3}$) 23, 24. Gauss's theorem is used to find electric fields; for instance, for an infinite wire, a cylindrical Gaussian surface is drawn where the area vector is in the same direction as the electric field, utilizing linear charge density ($\lambda$) 25, 26. Also covers electrostatic potential ($U = qV$) 23, equipotential surfaces, dielectrics, and capacitors in series and parallel 27, 28. If a dielectric is introduced into a medium, the force between charges becomes divided by the dielectric constant $K$ 29.
* Current Electricity: Examines electric current, mobility, and drift velocity, which explains that electrons don't just move directly but drift while undergoing thermal velocity 28, 30. Also covers Ohm's law, electrical resistivity, temperature dependence of resistance, Kirchhoff's rules, and the Wheatstone bridge, where equilibrium occurs when $P/Q = R/S$ 28, 31, 32.
* Magnetic Effects of Current: Covers the Biot-Savart law, Ampere's law, Oersted's experiment, and forces on moving charges 31, 33. Also explains moving coil galvanometers, bar magnets as equivalent solenoids, and magnetic properties of materials including paramagnetism, diamagnetism, and ferromagnetism 33, 34.
* Electromagnetic Induction and AC: Focuses on Faraday's laws, Lenz's Law, and self and mutual induction 34, 35. Alternating current topics include peak/RMS values, impedance, LCR series circuits, and transformers (a step-up transformer has $N_S > N_P$ resulting in $E_S > E_P$) 35, 36.
* Electromagnetic Waves: Identifies EM waves as produced by oscillating accelerated charges 37. Covers displacement current, Maxwell's equations, the transverse nature of EM waves, and the full electromagnetic spectrum from radio waves to gamma rays 35, 38, 39.
7. Optics
* Ray (Geometrical) Optics: Covers the reflection of light by plane mirrors, noting that image distance equals object distance and virtual images are formed for real objects 36, 39. Analyzes spherical mirrors, mirror formula, total internal reflection, optical fibers, lens maker’s formula, and optical instruments like microscopes and telescopes 39.
* Wave Optics: Based on Huygens' principle, it covers the reflection and refraction of plane waves 40, 41. Analyzes interference via Young's double-slit experiment and diffraction due to a single slit, where the maxima is found at $a \sin\theta = (2m + 1)\lambda/2$ 41, 42.
8. Modern Physics
* Dual Nature of Radiation: Moving particles behave like waves, a phenomenon observed in electron diffraction 43, 44. Includes the photoelectric effect based on Hertz and Lenard's observations, Einstein's photoelectric equation, and the de-Broglie relation 45, 46.
* Atoms and Nuclei: Outlines atomic models by J.J. Thomson (plum-pudding), Rutherford (planetary), and Niels Bohr 47, 48. In the Bohr model, the angular momentum of an orbiting electron is quantized 49. The total energy of an orbiting electron is negative (physically signifying it is bound to the nucleus), given by the formula $-13.6 \text{ eV}/n^2$ for Hydrogen 49, 50. Also explores nuclear force, mass defect, binding energy, isotopes, isobars, isotones, nuclear fission, and fusion 51, 52. Nuclear reactors utilize materials to slow down fast-moving neutrons and possess shutdown mechanisms for emergencies 49.
* Semiconductors: Analyzes energy bands in materials. If the forbidden energy band is absent, the material is a conductor (metal) 43, 44. Discusses intrinsic and extrinsic (p-type and n-type) semiconductors, p-n junctions, and various diodes like photodiodes, LEDs, rectifiers, and Zener diodes (used as voltage regulators) 49, 52-54.
